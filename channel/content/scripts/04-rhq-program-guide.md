## CHAPTER 1: What Is an RHQ? (0:30–2:30)

A Regional Headquarters is not just an office with a fancy plaque. Under Saudi regulations, an RHQ is the **strategic management hub** for a multinational group's operations across the MENA region.

**Core functions include:**
- Strategic direction and regional oversight  
- Budgeting and business planning for the region  
- Coordination between regional affiliates  
- Optional: shared services (HR, finance, legal) for the region  

**It is NOT:**
- A sales office only  
- A shell company with one employee  
- A replacement for every local subsidiary in each country

## CHAPTER 2: Why Saudi Is Pushing RHQ (2:30–4:00)

**[On camera]**

"Three drivers:

**1. Vision 2030 economic diversification** — Riyadh wants to be the business capital of the region, not just an oil economy.

**2. Government procurement rule** — From January 2024, companies without an RHQ in Saudi cannot contract with Saudi government entities (with limited exceptions). This is the stick.

**3. Tax incentives** — 30-year exemption on RHQ activities from corporate income tax. This is the carrot.

If you sell to Saudi government, semi-government, or state-linked enterprises, this program directly affects your revenue."

## CHAPTER 3: Benefits Package (4:00–6:00)

**[On camera + benefit cards on screen]**

"Qualifying RHQ entities receive:

| Benefit | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| **30-year tax holiday** | On eligible RHQ activities (not all group income) |
| **Saudization exemption** | For first 10 years (RHQ-specific roles) |
| **Expedited visas** | Including family and domestic worker visas |
| **Government contract eligibility** | Access to public sector tenders |
| **End-to-end government support** | Through RHQ Program Office |

**Important nuance:** The tax holiday applies to **RHQ activities** — income from operational subsidiaries, manufacturing, or local sales may still be taxed under normal rules. Structure matters."

---

## CHAPTER 4: Eligibility Requirements (6:00–9:00)

**[On camera]**

"To qualify, your group must generally meet:

**1. Multinational presence** — Operations in at least 2 other countries (outside Saudi and your home country) in the MENA region.

**2. Physical office in Saudi Arabia** — Dedicated RHQ office space in KSA (not a virtual office).

**3. Minimum headcount** — At least **15 full-time employees** at the RHQ within the first year, including:
   - Minimum **3 C-suite or senior executive roles** (e.g., regional CEO, CFO, COO)  
   - Remaining roles supporting RHQ functions  

**4. RHQ license from MISA** — Specific RHQ license category, distinct from standard investment license.

**5. Commitment to regional coordination** — Documented evidence that the RHQ performs strategic management functions.

**6. Timeline** — Establish within required window after license approval (verify current deadline on MISA portal).

**[On-screen: checklist with checkboxes]**

**Common disqualifier:** Hiring 15 people but all in admin roles with no genuine C-suite regional mandate. MISA reviews substance, not just headcount."

## CHAPTER 5: Setup Process (9:00–11:30)

**[On camera]**

"**Phase 1 — Decision & planning (Weeks 1–4)**
- Confirm group qualifies (2+ MENA countries)  
- Define RHQ scope and org design  
- Select office location (KAFD popular for RHQs)  
- Engage Saudi legal counsel  

**Phase 2 — MISA RHQ license (Weeks 4–8)**
- Apply via invest.sa for RHQ license category  
- Submit group structure chart, financials, business plan for RHQ  
- Receive RHQ investment license  

**Phase 3 — Entity formation (Weeks 8–12)**
- Commercial Registration for RHQ entity  
- ZATCA registration  
- Chamber of Commerce  
- Bank account  

**Phase 4 — Office & hiring (Months 3–12)**
- Lease and fit office  
- Recruit C-suite and RHQ team (15+ FTEs within year 1)  
- Register with RHQ Program Office  
- Obtain RHQ certificate  

**Phase 5 — Operations & compliance**
- Annual reporting to MISA and RHQ Program Office  
- Maintain headcount and substance requirements  
- Separate accounting for RHQ activities vs. other Saudi entities"

## CHAPTER 6: RHQ vs. Standard Subsidiary (11:30–13:00)

**[Split screen comparison]**

| | RHQ | Standard LLC |
|---|-----|--------------|
| Purpose | Regional management | Local commercial operations |
| Tax | 30-year holiday on RHQ activities | Standard CIT rates |
| Saudization | 10-year exemption (RHQ roles) | Full Nitaqat from start |
| Gov contracts | Eligible | Eligible (without RHQ restriction) |
| Headcount | 15+ with C-suite | Depends on operations |
| Best for | Multinationals with regional footprint | Companies selling locally in KSA |

"Many groups need **both**: an RHQ for regional strategy AND a subsidiary for local sales/delivery. They're complementary, not either/or."

## CHAPTER 8: Mistakes & Pitfalls (14:30–15:30)

1. Treating RHQ as a paperwork exercise with no real regional function  
2. Confusing RHQ tax holiday with all Saudi entity income  
3. Missing the 15-FTE deadline  
4. Not separating RHQ accounting from subsidiary operations  
5. Ignoring that gov contract eligibility requires active RHQ certificate  
6. Choosing cheap office that fails MISA inspection standards  
7. Hiring locally without a regional C-suite actually based in Riyadh"
