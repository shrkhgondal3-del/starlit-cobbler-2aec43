## CHAPTER 1: The Old Rule vs. Today's Reality (0:30–2:30)

**[On camera]**

"For decades, property ownership in Saudi Arabia was essentially limited to Saudi nationals and GCC citizens. Everyone else could lease — but not own freehold.

That changed as part of Vision 2030. The government recognized that attracting foreign investment, talent, and capital requires giving international buyers a path to ownership — at least in designated areas and through specific programs.

Today, there are **four main pathways** for non-Saudis to own property:

1. **GCC nationals** — broad ownership rights, similar to Saudi citizens in most cases  
2. **Premium Residency holders** — can own residential and commercial property in approved zones  
3. **Foreign nationals in designated development zones** — specific projects approved by REGA  
4. **Companies with foreign investment licenses** — commercial property through a Saudi entity  

We'll break down each one — because 'foreigner' doesn't mean one thing in Saudi law."

## CHAPTER 3: Premium Residency Route (3:45–6:30)

**[On camera]**

"The **Saudi Premium Residency** program is the most important pathway for high-net-worth individuals who want to live in or invest in Saudi Arabia long-term.

There are tiers — including an unlimited-duration option and a one-year renewable option. Holders can:

- Own residential and commercial real estate in approved areas  
- Live in the Kingdom without a traditional sponsor  
- Conduct business activities within the scope of their residency  

**Key requirements** typically include:
- Proof of financial solvency (minimum wealth thresholds apply — verify current figures on the official Premium Residency portal)  
- Clean criminal record  
- Valid passport  
- Application fee  

**Timeline:** Processing can take several weeks to a few months depending on application completeness.

**Important caveat:** Premium Residency gives you ownership rights in **approved zones** — not automatically every property in the Kingdom. Always verify the specific project or district before committing."

## CHAPTER 4: Designated Zones & Developer Projects (6:30–9:30)

**[On camera]**

"For foreigners who don't have Premium Residency, the practical route today is often through **REGA-approved developments** in designated zones.

Major giga-projects and master-planned communities — including developments linked to NEOM, the Red Sea Project, ROSHN communities, Diriyah Gate, and certain Riyadh/Jeddah projects — have been structured to allow foreign ownership, often off-plan.

**What you need to verify on every project:**

1. **Is foreign ownership explicitly permitted?** — Get it in writing from the developer AND confirm on REGA's registry  
2. **Freehold or leasehold?** — Some 'ownership' is actually long-term lease (usufruct). Know the difference  
3. **Escrow protection** — For off-plan, confirm funds are held in a regulated escrow account  
4. **Completion timeline** — Giga-projects have long horizons; model your cash flow accordingly  
5. **Resale restrictions** — Some projects limit resale for a period  

**[On-screen checklist animation]**

**Red flag:** Any agent or developer who says 'everyone can buy anywhere in Saudi' without qualifying the zone — walk away."

## CHAPTER 6: The Purchase Process — Step by Step (11:00–13:00)

**[On camera + screen share of process diagram]**

"Here's the typical flow for a qualifying foreign buyer:

**Step 1:** Confirm eligibility for the specific property and zone  
**Step 2:** Reserve unit and sign sale agreement  
**Step 3:** Deposit to escrow (off-plan) or transfer funds (ready property)  
**Step 4:** Obtain any required approvals (MISA, municipality, developer NOC)  
**Step 5:** Register title with **REGA** (Real Estate General Authority)  
**Step 6:** Register with municipality and utilities  

You'll need: valid passport, proof of funds, eligibility documentation (Premium Residency or foreign ownership approval), and potentially a local representative or lawyer.

**Costs to budget:**
- Property price (obviously)  
- Registration and transfer fees (typically a percentage of value — verify current REGA fee schedule)  
- Legal fees  
- Agent commission (if applicable)  
- VAT on commercial property (residential may be exempt — confirm with ZATCA guidance)

## CHAPTER 7: Financing & Mortgages (13:00–14:00)

**[On camera]**

"Can foreigners get a mortgage in Saudi Arabia? **In limited cases, yes** — but it's harder than in Dubai or Europe.

Saudi banks have historically prioritized lending to citizens and residents with stable local income. Premium Residency holders and those with established Saudi income streams have better options.

Most foreign buyers today purchase **cash or via international financing** secured against assets outside KSA. Don't assume local mortgage pre-approval until you've spoken to a Saudi bank with your specific residency status."

## CHAPTER 8: Common Mistakes (14:00–15:00)

**[On camera, faster pace]**

"Five mistakes I see constantly:

1. **Assuming Dubai rules apply** — Saudi is a separate legal system  
2. **Skipping REGA verification** — always check the title registry  
3. **Off-plan without escrow confirmation** — non-negotiable  
4. **Ignoring Premium Residency as a parallel track** — it may be cheaper than workarounds  
5. **No local legal counsel** — a Saudi property lawyer is not optional for six-figure decisions"
