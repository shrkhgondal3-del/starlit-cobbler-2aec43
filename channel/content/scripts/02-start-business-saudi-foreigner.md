## CHAPTER 1: Choose Your Path (0:30–3:00)

**[On camera + entity comparison graphic]**

"Before you file anything, answer one question: **What are you trying to do in Saudi Arabia?**

| Goal | Typical structure |
|------|-------------------|
| Test the market with limited activity | Branch of foreign company |
| Full operations, hiring, invoicing locally | Subsidiary (LLC) with MISA license |
| Multinational regional management hub | Regional Headquarters (RHQ) |
| Specific sector in a free zone | Free zone entity |

**Branch office:** Extension of your existing foreign company. Faster to set up, but limited — you generally can't do everything a local company can.

**Subsidiary (LLC):** A Saudi legal entity you own (fully or partially). Most common for serious market entry. Requires a **MISA foreign investment license** first.

**RHQ:** For companies with operations in 2+ Middle East/Africa countries who want to centralize regional management in Saudi Arabia. Comes with significant incentives — we have a dedicated video on this.

**Free zone:** Sector-specific zones with their own rules. Good for certain industries; not always ideal if your customers are on the Saudi mainland.

## CHAPTER 2: MISA — Your First Stop (3:00–6:00)

**[On camera]**

"For most foreign investors, everything starts with **MISA** — the Ministry of Investment. You need an investment license before you can register a Saudi company.

**The process:**

**Step 1 — Create account** on the MISA investor portal (invest.sa)

**Step 2 — Select activity** from the ISIC activity list. Your chosen activities determine requirements, minimum capital, and whether the sector is open, restricted, or prohibited to foreign investment.

**Step 3 — Submit application** with:
- Certificate of incorporation (apostilled/legalized from home country)  
- Articles of association  
- Audited financials (often required for established companies)  
- Board resolution authorizing Saudi establishment  
- Passport copies of shareholders and managers  
- Business plan (for certain activities)  

**Step 4 — MISA review** — typically 1–3 weeks for straightforward applications; longer for regulated sectors.

**Step 5 — Receive investment license** — this is your green light to proceed to Commercial Registration.

**[On-screen: MISA portal walkthrough — blur personal data]**

**Restricted activities:** Some sectors cap foreign ownership or require a Saudi partner. MISA's activity list shows the foreign ownership percentage allowed for each activity. Don't assume 100% ownership — verify your specific ISIC code."

---

## CHAPTER 3: Commercial Registration (CR) (6:00–8:30)

**[On camera]**

"With your MISA license in hand, you register your company with the **Ministry of Commerce** to get your **Commercial Registration (CR)** — think of it as your company's birth certificate.

**Steps:**
1. Reserve company name (Arabic and English)  
2. Draft and notarize Articles of Association  
3. Submit CR application via the Ministry of Commerce portal  
4. Pay registration fees  
5. Receive CR certificate with your unified national number  

**Timeline:** 1–2 weeks after MISA approval, assuming documents are clean.

**Your CR shows:** company name, activities, capital, managers, and registration number. You'll need it for everything — bank account, visas, contracts, tax registration."

---

## CHAPTER 4: Post-Registration Essentials (8:30–11:30)

**[On camera, checklist format]**

"CR is not the finish line. Here's what comes immediately after:

### 1. ZATCA Registration (Tax)
Register with the **Zakat, Tax and Customs Authority** for:
- Corporate income tax (if applicable to foreign-owned portions)  
- VAT registration (mandatory if turnover exceeds threshold — currently SAR 375,000)  
- Withholding tax obligations  

### 2. Chamber of Commerce
Join the relevant **Chamber of Commerce** — required for commercial operations and visa processing.

### 3. National Address
Register a **national address** — mandatory for all government interactions.

### 4. Bank Account
Open a **corporate bank account**. Saudi banks will require CR, MISA license, Articles, manager IDs, and often a minimum deposit. Timeline: 2–6 weeks (patience required).

### 5. GOSI Registration
If you're hiring, register with **GOSI** (social insurance) from day one of employment.

### 6. Qiwa / Ministry of HR
Set up on **Qiwa** portal for Saudization compliance and work permit issuance.

### 7. Municipal License
Depending on your activity, a **municipal license** for your physical premises.

**[On-screen: timeline graphic — Week 1 through Week 12]**

**Realistic total timeline:** 8–16 weeks from first MISA application to fully operational, assuming no sector-specific approvals needed."

## CHAPTER 5: Costs (11:30–13:00)

**[On camera + cost table]**

"Budget transparently. These are **indicative ranges** — verify before you commit:

| Item | Indicative range (SAR) |
|------|------------------------|
| MISA license fee | 2,000 – 10,000+ |
| CR registration | 1,200 – 3,000 |
| Legal/consulting fees | 15,000 – 80,000+ |
| Minimum capital (varies by activity) | 0 – 500,000+ |
| Chamber of Commerce | 2,000 – 10,000/year |
| Office lease (Riyadh, annual) | 80,000 – 500,000+ |
| Visa & iqama per employee | 5,000 – 15,000 |

**Hidden costs people forget:** document attestation and apostille in home country, PRO service fees, translation, and the operational cost of compliance (accounting, audit, Saudization).

**[Disclaimer on screen: 'Verify all fees with official portals']**"

## CHAPTER 6: Saudization & Hiring (13:00–14:30)

**[On camera]**

"You can't talk about Saudi business setup without **Saudization** — the Nitaqat system that requires companies to maintain a minimum percentage of Saudi national employees.

Requirements vary by:
- Company size  
- Sector  
- Nitaqat band (Platinum, Green, Yellow, Red)  

Foreign founders often underestimate this. Before you hire your first expat employee, understand your sector's Saudization target on the Qiwa portal.

**Practical tip:** Factor in Saudi hiring from month one of your business plan — not as an afterthought."

---

## CHAPTER 7: RHQ — When It Makes Sense (14:30–15:30)

**[On camera]**

"If you're a multinational with existing Middle East operations, the **Regional Headquarters program** deserves separate consideration.

Benefits include a 30-year tax holiday on RHQ activities for qualifying entities, expedited visas, and government contract eligibility.

Requirements include establishing a physical RHQ in Saudi Arabia, employing a minimum number of roles, and coordinating regional operations from KSA.

Full breakdown in our dedicated RHQ video — but mention it here because many founders choose RHQ over a standard subsidiary and regret not evaluating both."

---

## CHAPTER 8: Top 7 Mistakes (15:30–16:30)

**[On camera, rapid fire]**

1. Choosing the wrong ISIC activity code  
2. Underestimating document attestation time in home country  
3. No Saudi accountant from day one  
4. Ignoring Saudization in the business plan  
5. Signing office lease before CR is issued  
6. Assuming 100% foreign ownership without checking sector rules  
7. Using unlicensed 'consultants' who aren't registered with MISA"
